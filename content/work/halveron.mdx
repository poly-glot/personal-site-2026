<Chapter label="Strategy" title="Help operations leaders find the right person at Halveron, and get in touch." lede="Halveron works only with service organisations, in eight sectors across the UK. It judges its work by what remains when it leaves, so it trains clients' own people to keep improving without it, and it also runs a free forum for operations leaders. That gives the site two jobs: bring each of its four kinds of visitor to the right place, and stay easy for Halveron's own staff to keep up to date." split>
<Split reverse>
<Steps>
  <Step open name="The aim" hint="operations leaders get in touch">Winning means an operations leader finds their sector, reads how Halveron works there and what it changed for other organisations, and gets in touch. So every sector page carries an enquiry form, and so does the contact page. A leader can write to Halveron from the page they are reading, while what they have just read is still in front of them.</Step>
  <Step name="Where to play" hint="eight sectors, four kinds of visitor">The eight sectors run from banking and utilities to healthcare and the public sector, and Halveron offers eight capabilities, the kinds of work it does. Four kinds of visitor share its site: leaders looking for help, people choosing a course, members of its Lean Service Forum and job seekers. A leader can send an enquiry from the site. Asking about a course, a forum place or a job means sending Halveron an email.</Step>
  <Step name="How to win" hint="a named person beside the work">Halveron says it recruits people who have done the job before they advise on it, and it shares what it learns: its articles come from client work, and its forum is free. So the site shows that work and names a person beside it. Every capability, sector and course page shows who to talk to, with a portrait and a role, so a leader can see who they would deal with.</Step>
  <Step name="What it takes" hint="each person entered once">People change roles, clients come and go, and course and meeting dates move on, across 57 pages for four kinds of visitor. Halveron's staff have to keep up without a developer. So each person, client and article has to live in one place, where staff change it once, and every page that shows it has to follow.</Step>
  <Step name="How we'd know" hint="enquiries, and where each came from">Success would show in enquiries from leaders, and in which pages bring them in. So the two things to record are the form each enquiry came from and the page it was sent from, which would show which sectors and articles bring leaders to Halveron. This is a demonstration, so there are no figures. What can be checked today: every sector page names a person, and that person's details come from one record.</Step>
</Steps>

<Chapter label="Components" title="39 components build every page, including the box that names a person to talk to." lede="Putting a named person beside Halveron's work takes one box, and it appears on every capability, sector and course page, and on the forum and conference pages. Anything that might change, such as a person's role, is a field staff fill in. The list of those fields, 255 in all, is the content model. <Chapter label="The limits" title="Where the strategy is not proven yet." lede="The strategy measures success in enquiries and the pages that bring them in. Two of its rules are built in: code reaches the server only through a deploy, and each person is entered once. Each gap below is set against the part of the strategy it puts at risk.">
<List>
  <Item title="The aim: an enquiry waits until someone looks, and no white paper is sent.">Nothing in the site&apos;s code sends an email, so an enquiry waits in WordPress until someone opens it. The white-paper form says it will send the paper to the visitor&apos;s inbox, and its button reads Complete profile and download, but no file is attached and nothing is sent.</Item>
  <Item title="How to win: every sector page shows the same four case studies.">All eight sector pages list the same four case studies, from a water company, a bank, an insurer and a council, in a different order. A leader in healthcare, travel, education or managed services finds nothing from their own sector.</Item>
  <Item title="What it takes: past dates stay on the page until someone changes them.">Nothing compares a date with today. On 22 November 2026 the home page and the courses page still offer a course on 10 November, and the forum page still shows the meeting on 19 November as the next one.</Item>
  <Item title="The aim: accessibility has small faults and no automatic test.">Two of the outlines that show where the keyboard is fall below the 3:1 contrast the accessibility guidelines (WCAG) ask for, and the nine email links on the About page are smaller than the 24-pixel minimum. The accessibility statement says every page passes automated checks, but nothing in the repository runs one, and screen-reader testing has not been done.</Item>
  <Item title="How we'd know: there are no real enquiries to count.">The handler saves every enquiry with the form and the page it came from, but because Halveron is imaginary, the forms ask visitors not to enter real personal information. So the records it keeps cannot show which sectors or articles bring leaders in.</Item>
  <Item title="What it takes: the starting content comes back on every deploy.">A script called the seed fills the site on every deploy and resets every record, setting and menu it created, which suits a demonstration. It would even publish again a person staff had taken down. On a live site it would run once, at launch.</Item>
  <Item title="What it takes: hardening is unfinished.">The site holds visitors&apos; enquiries, but it sends no content security policy, a header that tells the browser which scripts to trust, and it still announces its WordPress and PHP versions.</Item>
</List>

<Chapter label="The lessons" title="Three things I would do the same way again." lede="The strategy set out to bring operations leaders to the right person at Halveron, on a site that also serves three other kinds of visitor and that Halveron's own staff keep current. These are the choices that made that work.">
<Split>
<List>
  <Item title="Start from what the firm says about itself.">Halveron says it judges itself by what remains when it leaves, and that its people have done the job before they advise on it. Those two lines decided the site map and the components: courses and the forum get sections of their own, and every capability, sector and course page names a person to talk to.</Item>
  <Item title="Enter each person once.">A person is one record, and other forms choose from it, such as a sector&apos;s contact or an article&apos;s author. Ruth Calloway, the managing director, appears on six pages, and her name, role and portrait come from her one form on all of them. Changing her role on a local copy changed it on all five of the pages that were checked.</Item>
  <Item title="Save each enquiry with the page it came from.">Every enquiry is saved with the form it came from and the page it was sent from, so staff can tell a leader who read the Utilities page from one who asked for a white paper. Each form carries two hidden values for this: its own name and the page&apos;s.</Item>
</List>
