<Chapter label="Strategy" title="Introduce investors to the people behind each strategy, so they get in touch." lede="Wealth is a small, partner-owned investment manager in St James's, London. It runs four investment strategies and closes each one to new money when it is full. It also promises investors that there is no sales team between them and the people who manage their money. So the site has two jobs: introduce those people, and stay easy for Wealth's own staff to keep up to date." split>

<Steps>
  <Step open name="The aim" hint="investors get in touch with the team">Winning means a professional investor reads about a strategy, sees who manages it and how they think, and then gets in touch. Every strategy, person and insight page ends with a Contact us button, and the contact page promises that a named person will reply, usually the portfolio manager of the strategy the investor asked about.</Step>
  <Step name="Where to play" hint="professional investors, four strategies">The site is for professional investors and the people who advise them. Its legal page says the strategies are not meant for retail investors, and sends private investors to a regulated financial adviser. Wealth runs four strategies, no more than it can manage well, and each has a page of its own. Nothing can be bought on the site. An investor's next step is a conversation with the firm.</Step>
  <Step name="How to win" hint="the people behind each strategy">Wealth promises that the people who manage an investor's money are the ones who answer their questions. So the site puts those people first. Every strategy says who manages it, each manager's page lists the strategies they run and the last insight, or article, they wrote, and every insight links to its author. An investor can see how the team thinks before the first meeting.</Step>
  <Step name="What it takes" hint="staff who keep it current">Who manages what, who works at the firm and what they have written all change over time, and Wealth's staff have to keep up without a developer. So each strategy, person and insight is a form they fill in. Who manages a strategy is entered once, on the strategy, and the author of an insight is entered once, on the insight. Each person's page then finds the strategies and insights that name them.</Step>
  <Step name="How we'd know" hint="enquiries, by type of investor">Success would show in enquiries, and in who sends them. The contact form asks each visitor what kind of investor they are, and the site keeps every enquiry as a private record that staff can sort by investor type. In this demonstration the form asks visitors not to send real details, so there are no figures. What can be checked today: every strategy names its managers, and every insight links to its author.</Step>
</Steps>

<Chapter label="The limits" title="Where the site's strategy is not proven yet." lede="The site's strategy measures success in enquiries from professional investors. Two of its rules are built in: new code reaches the server only through a deploy, and who manages a strategy is entered once, on the strategy. Each gap below is set against the part of that strategy it puts at risk.">
<List>
  <Item title="How we'd know: there are no real enquiries to count.">The form stores every enquiry, but because Wealth is imaginary the page asks visitors not to send real details. So the records it keeps cannot show which investors the site reaches.</Item>
  <Item title="The aim: nobody is told when an enquiry arrives.">The thank-you message promises a reply within two working days, but the site sends no email, so an enquiry waits until someone opens WordPress. The privacy notice also names an email service that the code does not use.</Item>
  <Item title="The aim: on a phone, the home page jumps while it loads.">In two of three Lighthouse phone tests, the home page&apos;s main photograph moved as it loaded, and the web fonts added a little more. That gave a layout shift of 0.347, where 0.1 or less counts as good.</Item>
  <Item title="Where to play: no page has a description for search engines.">Each of the 26 plain HTML pages has one, but the WordPress theme does not print it, so a search engine picks its own text for the result. That is why Lighthouse scores the site&apos;s search basics, labelled SEO, at 92.</Item>
  <Item title="How to win: the phone menu fails on five pages.">At 390 pixels wide, the menu on the home page, the three news category pages and Page not found opens behind the content below it, so some of its links cannot be tapped (on the home page, none of them can). No check runs before a deploy, and the accessibility statement still says there are no known barriers.</Item>
  <Item title="What it takes: the starting content comes back on every deploy.">A script called the seed fills the site on every deploy and rewrites each strategy, person, insight and page it created, which suits a demonstration. On a live site that would undo the staff&apos;s edits, so there the seed would run once, at launch.</Item>
  <Item title="What it takes: hardening is unfinished.">The site holds investors&apos; enquiries, but it sends no content security policy, a header that tells the browser which scripts to trust, and it still announces its WordPress and PHP versions.</Item>
</List>

<Chapter label="The lessons" title="Three things I would do the same way again." lede="The site's strategy set out to introduce professional investors to the people behind each of Wealth's four investment strategies, on a site the firm's own staff keep current without a developer. These are the choices that made both halves of that work.">
<Split>
<List>
  <Item title="Start from what the firm promises investors.">Wealth promises investors that they deal with the people who manage their money, with no sales team in between. That decided the site map, the components and the forms, so every strategy names its managers and every manager&apos;s page shows what they run and write.</Item>
  <Item title="Store who manages what in one place.">At first a person also had a Manages field, so the same fact lived in two places and could drift apart. I removed it on 2 October 2026, and each person&apos;s page now works the list out from the strategies.</Item>
  <Item title="Finish every page before WordPress.">All 26 pages were built as plain web pages first, so the fields came from finished pages and staff got a box for each thing that changes. The WordPress theme then reproduced each page as it was.</Item>
</List>
