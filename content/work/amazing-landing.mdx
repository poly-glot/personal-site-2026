<Chapter label="The challenge" title="From her screen to our counter." lede="Azadi launched a skincare range for women of all ages (under 25 to 55 and beyond) and sold it through its own boutiques. The success was all in one shape really: a woman at the counter with her voucher, collecting her free trial. Everything online was designed to get her to the counter.">
<Rows>
  <Row title="Earn the journey">A click costs nothing. A trip to a boutique costs an afternoon. The page had to make that visit feel worth the effort before it asked her for anything.</Row>
  <Row title="Recommend one product">The quiz stands in for a conversation at the counter. Three questions on her age, her skin and her concerns lead to one product chosen for her, never a catalogue.</Row>
  <Row title="Name the nearest door">A recommendation is wasted if the boutique is an hour away. Her town is matched to the closest participating store, which is named on the voucher and in the email.</Row>
  <Row title="Personalise the voucher">The voucher is the last impression before the shop. It greets her by name, gives the boutique's address and the product to collect, and adds £10 off skincare, redeemed on collection.</Row>
</Rows>
</Chapter>

<List>
  <Item title="Colour is the navigation.">Each product owns a colour. The landing page shows all four side by side, and the result page turns into the colour of the product you matched.</Item>
  <Item title="The diamond asks the questions.">It starts as the mark in the logo, then becomes the answer tile, its selected state and the marker above each question.</Item>
  <Item title="Paper holds the promise.">The trial form sits on a paper card with stepped corners, set apart from the flat colour around it.</Item>
  <Item title="Motion carries you forward.">Pages hand over in short sequences, not cuts. Tiles arrive one at a time, and the product is revealed before its description.</Item>
</List>

<Chapter label="The build" title="Same pixels. New engine." lede="Put the 2015 and 2026 versions side by side and they look the same. That was the point. Everything underneath changed.">
<Eras>
  <Era period="2015" title="A jQuery campaign page" meta="23 scripts · 464 KB of JavaScript" src="/img/work/amazing-landing/landing-2015.webp" w={1280} h={800} alt="The 2015 landing page: four bands of colour with one product in each.">
    <Point tag="Scripts">Ten libraries, then thirteen page scripts.</Point>
    <Point tag="Motion">Velocity.js on top of jQuery.</Point>
    <Point tag="Entry">One script per page, wired together through globals.</Point>
  </Era>
  <Era period="2026" title="One module and a Go service" meta="One ES module · 47 KB of JavaScript" src="/img/work/amazing-landing/landing.webp" w={1280} h={800} alt="The 2026 landing page, which looks the same as the 2015 one.">
    <Point tag="Rollout">Both versions ship from one build and one server. A promotion picks which it uses, so the original keeps running untouched.</Point>
    <Point tag="Scripts">One ES module, with a class per page and a page router.</Point>
    <Point tag="Motion">The Web Animations API, behind one small helper.</Point>
    <Point tag="Edge">Firebase Hosting serves the quiz as static files and holds the four read endpoints for 30 days, so a cold start is paid by one visitor rather than by everyone.</Point>
    <Point tag="Server">Go's standard library on Cloud Run, with no framework. The route table is small enough not to need one.</Point>
    <Point tag="Tenancy">Every promotion has a slug, and the quiz loads its configuration by that slug. A new client is new data, not new code.</Point>
    <Point tag="Data">Cloud Datastore and encrypted cookie sessions: no schema to migrate and no session store to run.</Point>
    <Point tag="Tests">Eleven Playwright suites walk both versions, both screen sizes, the admin and the full journey.</Point>
  </Era>
</Eras>
</Chapter>

<Split>
<List>
  <Item title="Rebuild beside, not over.">The old version stayed live and selectable throughout. Nothing had to be switched off to ship the new one.</Item>
  <Item title="Let data carry the variants.">Promotions, questions and stores are rows, not branches. One build serves every campaign.</Item>
  <Item title="Cache what rarely changes.">A quiz's questions barely move, so they are held at the edge. Most of a visit never touches the server.</Item>
</List>
