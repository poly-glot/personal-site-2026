Its customers are women of all ages, and a woman of 25 wants something different from a woman of 55. <Steps>
  <Step open name="The aim" hint="a woman at the counter">Winning means a woman walks into a boutique with her voucher and collects her free trial. The voucher also takes £10 off skincare when she collects it, so the visit can turn into a sale. Every screen of the quiz leads to that visit.</Step>
  <Step name="Where to play" hint="women of all ages, near a boutique">The promotion is for women from under 25 to 55 and over who live near one of the eight boutiques in Britain and Ireland. It does not sell online. The quiz has one goal, a visit to the counter.</Step>
  <Step name="How to win" hint="one product and one boutique">A catalogue asks her to choose. The quiz asks three questions, the way an adviser at the counter would, and recommends one product. Her town then finds the nearest boutique. The voucher names both, so she knows what to ask for and where to go.</Step>
  <Step name="What it takes" hint="staff who change the promotion">Boutiques open and close, products change and questions get refined while a promotion runs. Azadi&apos;s staff have to make those changes and see every entry without waiting for a developer. And the 2015 version had to keep running while its replacement was built.</Step>
  <Step name="How we'd know" hint="trials collected at each boutique">Success would show as free trials collected at each boutique, against the entries that named it. Every entry records the product and the boutique and exports as a spreadsheet. Nothing yet records a voucher being collected, and Azadi is imaginary, so there are no figures.</Step>

<Chapter label="The limits" title="Where the strategy is not proven yet." lede="Each gap is set against the part of the strategy it puts at risk. The one that matters most is the time a change in the admin takes to reach her, because the strategy relies on staff changing the promotion while it runs.">
<List>
  <Item title="What it takes: a change in the admin can take 30 days to reach her.">The four answers the quiz reads (the promotion, the boutiques, the products and the questions) are held at the edge for 30 days. Only a new release of the hosting clears them, and a deploy of the server does not. So a boutique paused in the admin can still be offered for a month, unless a developer releases the hosting again.</Item>
  <Item title="What it takes: one set of questions, products and boutiques for every promotion.">A promotion holds its name, its web address name and a switch, and nothing else. A second client would share Azadi&apos;s questions, products and boutiques. The quiz also checks only that the promotion exists, so switching one off does not stop it.</Item>
  <Item title="How to win: the voucher says it was emailed, and no email is sent.">The server writes the voucher email and saves it for the admin, but nothing sends it. The voucher page tells her &lsquo;We have emailed your voucher to you&rsquo;, and it still says to collect by 2 November 2015, a date typed into the page that year.</Item>
  <Item title="How we'd know: nothing records a voucher being collected.">Every entry names its product and boutique and exports as a spreadsheet, so the visits each boutique should expect can be counted. The voucher carries no code for the counter, so the visits that happened cannot.</Item>
  <Item title="Where to play: a phone that opens the main address gets the desktop quiz.">The main address always sends a visitor to the desktop version, which a phone shows cropped. The phone version has its own address and nothing switches to it. On a phone, the result and voucher pages are also wider than the screen and scroll sideways.</Item>
  <Item title="The aim: links leave for addresses this project does not own.">The logo, the privacy and terms links and the product links go to azadi-cosmetics.com and azadi.com, which belong to nobody connected with this imaginary brand. The boutique&apos;s photo on the result page never loads, and an empty box stands in its place.</Item>
</List>
</Chapter>

<Chapter label="The lessons" title="Three things I would do the same way again." lede="The strategy set out to bring women into the boutiques for a free trial chosen for them, with a promotion Azadi&apos;s staff run themselves. These three choices did most of that work, and each still has a gap I would close.">
<Split>
<List>
  <Item title="Rebuild beside the original, not over it.">The 2015 quiz kept running at its own address, from the same build, so nothing had to be switched off to ship the new one. It also kept a working reference: the 2015 version still recommended the right product when a fault in the 2026 one sent every answer to Bloom, until I found and fixed it while writing this study. Next time the Playwright suites that walk both versions would run before every deploy.</Item>
  <Item title="Keep what staff change in the admin.">The boutiques, products and questions are records, not code, so one build serves both versions on both screens, and staff change them without a developer. Next time they would belong to a promotion, so a second client could have its own.</Item>
  <Item title="Hold the answers that rarely change at the edge, and clear them when staff save.">Most of a visit never reaches the server, so a slow start on Cloud Run is paid by one visitor rather than everyone. The same 30 days also hide a change in the admin. Next time a save in the admin would clear the four cached answers, so a change reaches her straight away.</Item>
</List>
